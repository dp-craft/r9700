## Summary

δ 0 (stock, bit-exact acceptance) is the only usable setting on this workload. It wrote the blind review in 2,182 s, 23% faster than
the same template on Sly 1.8.1 (2,819 s), and scored 21/38 against that run's 22/38, a tie under the pre-registered Δ ≥ 5 rule
(MEASURED). δ 0.3 decoded 51% faster but read the Opus review before writing its own, so the blind step never happened (FAILED).
δ 0.5 decoded 65% faster per token, yet needed 2.4× the wall time. It generated 4.1× the tokens, and 49% of them sat in 7 thinking
loops; one loop ran to the 64,000-token output cap (MEASURED). Its review was also worse: 16/38 against 21 (δ 0) and 22 (anchor) in the same blind pass, a 5-point drop that meets the
pre-registered Δ ≥ 5 rule exactly. It wrote 8 findings against 14-15 and missed a high one. Caveat: n = 1 per arm, so δ 0.3's protocol
skip is one event, not a rate.

## Consequences & root causes

1. **Faster decode does not mean faster work on a long agentic run.** MEASURED: δ 0.5 decodes 65% faster but spends 2.4× the wall,
   because it generated 491,925 tokens against 120,001. 239,521 of them were inside the 7 loop blocks. At 92 tok/s those are about
   2,600 s, roughly the whole wall gap of 3,155 s (INFERRED, assumes the loop tokens ran at the median decode rate). → *Judge Cactus on
   step wall time and output tokens, never on decode tok/s.*
2. **δ 0.5 also cost quality.** MEASURED: 16/38 against 21 for δ 0 in the same blind pass. It wrote 8 findings against 15 and lost
   one high, although it made more tool calls (131 vs 81). The cause is not established. Two candidates, both INFERRED: the lossy
   acceptance itself (δ is a per-token divergence budget from the target distribution), or loop output crowding the context of the
   later steps. With n = 1 the drop sits exactly on the Δ ≥ 5 threshold (OPEN until a second run). → *Speed from δ 0.5 is paid
   twice on this workload: in wall time and in findings.*
3. **The loops are the Cactus mechanism, not this template.** MEASURED onsets of the three largest δ 0.5 blocks: ~571, ~3,247 and
   ~8,066 output tokens. Two of them began before the fade starts at 4,096, at full δ. One ran to `max_tokens` 64,000; the other two
   escaped after 46.7k and 39.1k tokens, 23-30k tokens after δ had faded to 0. Mechanism (INFERRED from the code): under the 1.9.x cap
   the accept probability of a drafted token is `min(1, p + sqrt(2δp(1−p)))` capped at `1 − 0.5(1−p)`, so a confident repetition is
   accepted far more often than stock and keeps only half its escape rate. Full derivation:
   `docs/findings/agentic/2026-10-31-1745-reasoning-replay-sharp-template-cactus-loops.md` §5. → *δ 0 is the default; any δ > 0 needs an
   early fade and a stronger cap (P3 in that doc) before it is retried.*
4. **δ 0.3 is not a safe middle.** MEASURED: no loop in its 514 s, but it skipped the blind step. "Now let me read the Opus review
   for W2-08 (step B)" came before any review was written. With one run this cannot be pinned on δ (INFERRED: it could be sampling
   noise). Under the cap, δ 0.3 accepts exactly like δ 0.5 for every drafted token with target p ≥ 0.29, which is where loops live
   (INFERRED, §5 acceptance table). → *Do not promote δ 0.3.*
5. **δ 0 on 1.9.1 holds the quality of 1.8.1.** MEASURED: 21 vs 22/38 with equal highs (4/6). The 1-point gap is one partial match
   (the grader notes 19 without it) and is inside the Δ ≥ 5 noise rule. 1.9.1 is the base for the next arms.
6. **Memory is not the limit here.** MEASURED: peak VRAM 32,057-32,433 MiB at gpu util 0.93, peak GTT within ~100 MiB of the idle
   reading in every arm, so none of the outcomes above is a spill artefact.

## Methodology & caveats

- One run per arm (n = 1). The arms ran in sequence on fresh servers and fresh worktrees of the same base; prompt and RUNBOOK identical.
- Stop rule: the session is stopped at its first read of the Opus review (`stop_when.txt` per arm), so "wall" covers step A only.
- δ 0.3's FAILED status comes from its last actions: no `W2-08.refine-27b.md` and no probe directory when step B started.
- The blind grader saw anonymised labels only (`make_blind.py` redacts worktree paths and engine/model names and refuses to finish on a
  leak). The planned single 4-way pass was split because the sharp-sly2 arm was still running: this 3-way pass re-grades δ 0 and
  the anchor next to δ 0.5, and the anchor sits in every pass as the calibration constant.
- The power samples are instantaneous `amdgpu` readings; the mean is the comparable figure.
