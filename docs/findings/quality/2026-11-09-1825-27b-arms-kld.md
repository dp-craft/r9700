Arm a (Quark-MXFP4 on radiance 1.11.0) does 101.6 / 90.1 tok/s at 10k / 100k. IQ4R (c, c0) sits next to UD-Q4_K_XL (median per-position KL 1.5e-4), and Quark-MXFP4 (a, b) forms the other. Spec decode (c vs c0, 3e-6) and the radiance 1.11→1.17 upgrade (a vs b, 1.5e-5) are near-lossless. **Main caveat:** both sly-vLLM arms (d and e) differ from every other arm from the first generated tokens, and the cause is OPEN. With 16 prompts and greedy outputs that diverge after a median of 2–11 tokens, the KLD numbers are a preliminary ranking, not a final quality verdict.

## What is KLD, in plain words

**The idea.**
Before a model writes each word (token), it scores every word it could pick next: "`return` 70%, `if` 20%, `for` 5%, …".
A quantized model (a compressed copy) should give almost the same scores as the original.
KLD (Kullback–Leibler divergence) is one number that says how far apart two of those score lists are.

**How to read it.**
0 means both models think exactly alike at that step. Bigger means they disagree more.
It is counted in "nats per token": 0.001 is tiny, 0.01 is noticeable, 0.1 or more means the models often pick different words.
It is an average over many steps, so a few big disagreements can pull it up.

**How we measure it here, step by step.**
1. We take fixed text snippets, mostly code (the hash-pinned calibration set in `inputs/`: the first 16 of 128 in the run so far, all 128 in the next batch).
2. Each model gets the same snippet with no chat template, temperature 0 (always the most likely word), and writes up to 512 tokens.
3. At every step the server returns its top 20 candidate words with their scores (`logprobs: 20`).
4. We compare the two score lists at the same step and compute the KL for that step. Words outside the top 20 are counted with a small floor value, so the result is a lower bound: the real gap can only be a bit larger.
5. We average over all compared steps and prompts, and get a 95% range by resampling whole prompts (cluster bootstrap).

**Two ways to line up the steps.**
- *Free-running (this report so far):* both models write their own text. Once they pick different words, the rest of the text is different, so only the steps before that point are compared fairly. If two models split after 2 words, there is little to compare.
- *Teacher-forced (next batch):* the reference model (Q8_0, the near-original copy) writes the text once. Every other model is then shown the same snippet plus the reference's first *i* words and asked only for the next word's scores, at 32 evenly spaced points per snippet. Every model is judged on exactly the same contexts, so the numbers line up across models. Speed tricks such as DFlash or MTP do not change these scores, so their effect is measured separately, on free-running text.

**What KLD does not tell you.**
It measures closeness to the reference, not whether the code is correct. A small KLD means "behaves like the original"; task scores (tests passing) are a separate measurement.

## Consequences & root causes

1. **Spec decode and the radiance 1.17 upgrade are safe for quality.** c vs c0 gives median per-position KL 3e-6 with 99.7% paired top-1, and first divergence comes late (median position 46). a vs b gives 1.5e-5 with 98.6% paired top-1. Both are MEASURED. In theory, spec decode with greedy verification is exact (INFERRED). The residual difference is consistent with batch-shape numerics: the verify forward computes tokens in a different batch shape.
2. **The quant family matters more than the engine.** IQ4R on radiance (c) and UD-Q4_K_XL on llama.cpp (q4kxl) are closer to each other (median KL 1.5e-4, paired top-1 99.2%) than either is to Quark-MXFP4 on the same radiance engine (4.4e-4 to 1.5e-3). MEASURED. The data does not say *which* family is closer to the full-precision model. That needs the FP8 reference (`KLD_FP8=1`), which this run did not include.
3. **Both sly-vLLM arms differ from everything else from the first tokens.** d and e diverge at a median of position 1–2 against every radiance and llama.cpp arm. Their medians are 0.008–0.028, at least 50× the within-family values (3e-6 to 1.6e-4). MEASURED. The token spellings were checked and are identical across all three engines (`'\n'`, `'OUN'`, digits), so this is **not** a token-format artifact. The sly arms' distributions are visibly flatter: on prompt cal-002, the first-token logprob is −0.32 on d and −0.28 on e, against −0.05 to −0.10 on a, c and q4kxl. MEASURED. d and e are also closer to each other (0.016) than to anything else, although e is a different fine-tune. That points to something the two sly arms share rather than to the weights. INFERRED. Candidates include d's FP8/INT4 lm-head (whether e's profile sets it is not checked) and sly-side sampling or logprob post-processing. **OPEN**, and to be resolved before using d's KLD as a quality verdict.
4. **The pooled KL mean is not comparable across pairs that diverge early.** Each pair's mean is pooled over its paired positions (`cluster_bootstrap_ci`). When most prompts diverge at positions 0–4, the one prompt that stays aligned for 500 tokens dominates the average. That's why a · d shows 0.11 (556 positions, mostly from one prompt) while a · b (≈ a) vs d shows 0.57. The divergent position itself, included by design, contributes most of the mean: the means of 0.1–1.0 sit against medians of 1e-4–3e-2. Both observations are MEASURED, and the consequence is INFERRED. **For ranking, use the per-position median and paired top-1. The KL mean and its CI only hold for near-identical pairs (c·c0, a·b, c·q4kxl).** A teacher-forced score would fix this: score each arm on the reference's tokens. That would be a `kld_probe.py` extension and is not built.
5. **For decode speed, radiance with spec decode wins this single-stream workload. vLLM DFlash wins prefill but its drafter barely accepts.** DFlash acceptance was 23.0% at 10k and 12.5% at 100k (MEASURED, `spec` rows), so d's decode (79.8 / 53.9) is far below a's (101.6 / 90.1). Without spec decode, radiance c0 beats llama.cpp by 24% / 18% at decode and about 2.6× at prefill (MEASURED). llama.cpp's slow 100k TTFT (139.5 s, against 48–52 s on radiance) is the practical cost for agentic long-context use. INFERRED.
6. **The llama.cpp arm shows 1100 MiB peak GTT against a ~341–355 MiB baseline on the other arms.** MEASURED. That is about 750 MiB more GPU-accessible system RAM than the other arms, and rule 8 says to flag it. Likely cause: Vulkan host-visible staging and output buffers (INFERRED, OPEN). Decode (24.6–27.4) matches what a bandwidth-bound dense 27B without spec decode should do, so no slowdown from spilling is visible (INFERRED).

## Methodology & caveats

1. **The KLD is preliminary.** It covers 16 prompts (`KLD_N=16`), against 128 in the full calibration set, and is pairwise (arm vs arm). There is no full-precision reference (`KLD_FP8=0`), so it shows **which arms agree, not which is correct**.
2. **Early end of sequence:** 5–6 of the 16 prompts end after ≤2 generated tokens on every arm (cal-001/005/006/009/011/015; cal-001 not on a or b). This leaves fewer positions to score.
3. **Short top-k rows:** radiance often returns fewer than 20 entries per position (`dict-map[19]`, minimum 1, in 16/16 prompts on a, b, c and c0). d and e have short rows in 5–6 prompts, and llama.cpp none. Tokens the reference has that are missing from the arm's top-k are scored at the arm's floor. The KL is a lower bound, and it is looser when the arm is radiance.
4. **Arm d:** speed n=1. Rep 1 of both cells returned 1 token (`no tokens received`, usage `completion_tokens: 1`), was recorded as `n_err=1` and excluded from the aggregate. The cause is not investigated (**OPEN**). The first batch's KLD fetch for d was refused by the old lm-head check (still listed in `failures.txt`). It was re-fetched on the resume in a fresh boot (`server-d-redhat-sly1100-boot2.txt`) under the narrowed check, which allows the FP8/INT4 head because it returns full-row logits.
5. **Residency warning for q4kxl** (`failures.txt`, 0.00 GiB): `amd-smi process` shows no process for the Vulkan llama-server. The VRAM sampler (26404–26930 MiB) is the MEASURED usage, so the warning is a gap in the residency tool, not contention.
6. **Two depths only (10k, 100k), REPS=2, one stream.** There is no depth curve (CLAUDE.md rule 16), so no exponent is quoted and there's no ranking claim between those depths or beyond them. Radiance decode with spec decode varies with content (c: 44.0 at 10k vs 74.1 at 100k), so decode Δ under ~±20% at n=2 is not resolvable.
7. **Fixed across arms:** same fixtures (`codereview-10000.txt`, `codereview-100000.txt`), `/v1/completions`, `PREFIX_MODE=unique`, 512 generated tokens, fp8 KV (q8_0 on llama.cpp, which has no fp8 KV), ctx 260000, 2 sequences (e: 8192 / 1). Power: prefill 201–216 W average, decode 200–215 W average, all under the 210 W-class cap (`power` rows).
8. **Not run:** the q6k arm (dropped by the user) and the FP8 reference (optional).
