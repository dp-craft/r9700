## 1. Summary

**What was measured**
Fifteen arms were each scored against one reference (eleven in the first batch, d181 in round 2, q6k, q6kf16 and q4kxlf16 in round 3): unsloth Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 on llama.cpp (f16 KV, greedy). Each arm was teacher-forced on the reference's own continuation. That gives 128 calibration-v2 prompts × 32 positions = 4096 identical scored positions per arm, with 0 prompt errors in every arm (MEASURED, `kldf2-*.json`, `n_positions`/`n_prompt_errors`). The KL is a top-20 approximate lower bound in nats per token.

**Closest to Q8_0: Q6, then IQ4R-L-V4, then Q4_K_XL (round 3 added Q6)**
UD-Q6_K on llama.cpp (q6k, production q8_0 KV) is the closest production-config arm (its f16-KV twin q6kf16 is not resolved from it): KL 0.0045 [0.0030, 0.0063], top-1 99.0% (MEASURED, `kldf2-q6k`). It is resolved above the llama.cpp floor (+0.0027 [+0.0018, +0.0038], `diff2-q6k-q8f`) and below arm c: c − q6k = +0.0035 [+0.0019, +0.0054], c lower in only 12.5% of prompts (MEASURED, `diff2-c-q6k`). So **IQ4R-L-V4 is not "as close as Q6"**: it sits between Q6 and Q4_K_XL (INFERRED from the two resolved diffs).

IQ4R-L-V4 on radiance 1.17.0 (arm c) is next: KL 0.0079 [0.0055, 0.0107], top-1 agreement 98.7%. Next is UD-Q4_K_XL on llama.cpp (q4kxl): 0.0164 [0.0095, 0.0251], top-1 98.1%. All MEASURED (`kldf2-c`, `kldf2-q4kxl`). The paired difference resolves c as lower: −0.0084 [−0.0161, −0.0025], with c lower in 71% of prompts (MEASURED, `diff2-c-q4kxl`). The two arms differ in quant, engine and KV type at once.

**The MXFP4 family is about 7–8× further away than arm c, whatever the engine**
Six MXFP4 arms land between 0.056 and 0.066, with top-1 agreement of 95.3–96.1% (MEASURED, `kldf2-{a,b,d,d0,dfh,d181}`):
- Quark-MXFP4 on radiance (a, b);
- RedHat-MXFP4 on Sly vLLM 1.11.0 (d, d0, dfh) and on Sly 1.8.1 (d181).

c beats d by −0.056 [−0.075, −0.040], and c is lower in all 128 prompts (MEASURED, `diff2-c-d`). Radiance and vLLM on MXFP4 weights do not separate: b − d0f is −0.0032 [−0.0131, +0.0076], not resolved (MEASURED, `diff2-b-d0f`). So the 4-bit format drives the KL, not the engine (INFERRED). Published tables also put MXFP4 behind K-quant and affine 4-bit quants of similar size (CLAIMED, [§7.8](#78-external-context-claimed-tagged-as-in-the-methodology-research)).

**Noise floors**
- **llama.cpp:** a Q8_0 re-run (q8f) scores 0.0018 [0.0007, 0.0033] (MEASURED, `kldf2-q8f`).
- **vLLM:** a re-boot (d0f vs d0) differs by +0.000004 (MEASURED, `diff2-d0f-d0`), and 4083 of 4096 rows are bit-identical (MEASURED, raw local file, not committed: recount of `tmp/runs/forced-20261112-093746/results/kld-d0.jsonl` vs `kld-d0f.jsonl`; [§12.6](#126-numbers-derived-from-raw-local-files-not-committed)).
- **Radiance:** a re-boot (bf vs b) is bit-identical, 0.0 [0, 0] (MEASURED, `diff2-bf-b`).

Each arm's distance above the llama.cpp floor is resolved for q6k (+0.0027), c (+0.0062), q4kxl (+0.0146) and d (+0.062) (MEASURED, `diff2-*-q8f`).

**The q8_0 KV cache shows no cost (round 3)**
Same weights, f16 KV (the reference's KV) instead of the production q8_0: at Q6, q6kf16 − q6k = −0.00003 [−0.00031, +0.00031], not resolved; at Q4_K_XL, q4kxlf16 − q4kxl = +0.0009 [+0.0002, +0.0018], resolved, but in the opposite direction to a KV cost: the f16 arm is the further one, on 51% of prompts (MEASURED, `diff2-q6kf16-q6k`, `diff2-q4kxlf16-q4kxl`). The f16 arms also ran ctx 32768 / 1 slot instead of 260000 / 2, so that small Q4 difference belongs to the two changes together; either way, q8_0 KV is not further from the reference than f16 (INFERRED). The weights carry the distance: Q4_K_XL vs Q6 at the same f16 KV is +0.0128 [+0.0061, +0.0215] (MEASURED, `diff2-q4kxlf16-q6kf16`).

**DFlash is at noise level; MTP could not be measured**
Teacher forcing cannot see drafting. The forced d − d0 difference is +0.0031 [−0.0002, +0.0065], not resolved (MEASURED, `diff2-d-d0`). In free-running greedy output (16 prompts), DFlash on vs off gives KL 0.0059 [0.0028, 0.0144] with 99.0% paired top-1 (MEASURED, `diag-d-vs-d0.json`). llama.cpp b11448 returns no logprobs for MTP-accepted tokens, so MTP was not measurable by KL (CLAIMED, llama.cpp source per the engine audit).

**The int4 lm head costs 0.0045 nats/tok**
Turning off the int4 lm head on d0 (arm dfh) lowers KL by −0.0045 [−0.0058, −0.0032]. That is resolved, with dfh lower in 80% of prompts (MEASURED, `diff2-dfh-d0`). It accounts for about 7% of d0's distance from Q8_0 (INFERRED, ratio of MEASURED means).

**Engine upgrades, and where heretic's extra distance comes from (round 2)**
On the same `.rad`, a − b (radiance 1.11.0 minus 1.17.0) is +0.0023 [−0.0007, +0.0054], not resolved; the newer engine is nominally lower (MEASURED, `diff2-a-b`). The heretic model on Sly 1.8.1 (e) is further away than d: +0.025 [+0.009, +0.042], resolved (MEASURED, `diff2-e-d`). That first comparison mixes the model with the engine version.

Round 2 separates them. Arm **d181** is arm d's exact boot (RedHat-MXFP4, profile `sly-redhat/redhat-mxfp4-dflash`, DFlash k=7, ctx 260000 / 2 seqs, fp8 KV) on the old Sly `1.8.1-rocm10.1` image that e used. In the recorded server settings only the image (and its cache dir) differs from d (MEASURED, diff of the two `server-d-redhat-sly1100.txt`). d181 scores **0.0662** [0.0476, 0.0878], top-1 95.3% (MEASURED, `kldf2-d181`).

| Pair | What differs | Δ KL nats/tok | CI95 | Verdict |
|---|---|--:|---|---|
| d181 − d | Sly 1.8.1 minus 1.11.0, same model and settings | +0.0024 | [−0.0033, +0.0088] | not resolved |
| e − d181 | heretic vs RedHat-MXFP4, same Sly 1.8.1 | **+0.0225** | [+0.0055, +0.0388] | **d181 lower** (e lower in only 22.7% of prompts) |

MEASURED, `diff2-d181-d`, `diff2-e-d181`. So the Sly engine version has no measurable effect, and with the engine held equal the heretic model is still further from Q8_0 (INFERRED from the two diffs). "The model" here means the Swift-1.5 heretic fine-tune together with its own MXFP4-GPTQ quantization; these arms cannot split those two (INFERRED). e also differs from d181 in ctx 8192 / 1 seq, Cactus 0.5 and chat template, all INFERRED irrelevant to forced KLD ([§7.6](#76-engine-logprob-audit-results)). d181 also stays inside the MXFP4 cluster: d181 − d0f is +0.0056 [−0.0001, +0.0122] and d181 − b is +0.0087 [−0.0019, +0.0194], neither resolved (MEASURED, `diff2-d181-d0f`, `diff2-d181-b`).

**What this does not say**
- ⚠ **128 prompts is a small sample, from a narrow domain.** Every forced-KLD number here comes from 128 prompts × 32 positions (the free-running greedy diagnostics use 16 prompts): 45 TypeScript and 45 Python source files from two codebases, 14 English Markdown reports from this repo, and 24 English agentic-coding chat turns ([§7.7](#77-calibration-v2-what-exactly-is-tested)). No other programming language, no non-English text, no math, nothing above 4681 prompt tokens. The CIs (cluster bootstrap by prompt) already price the prompt-to-prompt spread, but they cannot cover domains that were never sampled. The ranking is measured on this mostly TS/Python coding mix (90 of 128 prompts are code); it has not been split by kind, so whether it holds in each kind, or outside this mix, is not known. Run the broader tests in [§2.2](#22-decision-guide) before generalising it.
- KLD measures closeness to Q8_0, not task correctness. Near the baseline, KLD stops predicting task scores (CLAIMED, arXiv 2606.19558).
- Contexts here are short: prompts of 246–4681 tokens (MEASURED, raw local file, not committed: `tmp/runs/forced-20261112-093746/results/kld-q8.jsonl` `prompt_tokens`). Depth was not tested.
- Speed is out of scope. It moves to a separate campaign ([§10](#10-limitations-and-next-steps)).
- The numbers are **production-config** distances: weights + KV-cache type + engine together (f16 KV on the reference, q8_0 on q4kxl and q6k, fp8 on every Radiance and Sly arm). Only q6kf16 and q4kxlf16 are weights-only. Even those are not directly comparable with published quant tables such as Unsloth's ([§7.9](#79-comparison-with-unsloths-published-kld-claimed)). The fp8-KV share of c and the MXFP4 arms is still unmeasured: their engines do not run f16 KV in production.

**How Δ vs the floor was computed.**
- ᵖ marks a **paired** difference with its own cluster-bootstrap CI (MEASURED, `kld-diffs.d/diff2-<arm>-q8f.json`). All four such differences are resolved.
- ᵃ marks a plain **arithmetic** difference of two MEASURED means. It has no CI. Use the paired table ([§12](#12-appendix)) to judge whether a difference is resolved.

**How to read the floors.**
- **q8f** is the floor for "is this arm distinguishable from Q8_0 at all".
- **d0f** and **bf** are not distances from Q8_0. They show that **re-booting** vLLM or radiance changes nothing (d0f − d0 = +0.000004; bf − b = 0). Their own rows therefore just repeat d0 and b.

**Grouping (INFERRED from the paired diffs).** The arms fall into three groups:
1. **near-Q8:** q6k, q6kf16 (not resolved from each other), then c, then q4kxl and q4kxlf16, each step resolved;
2. **MXFP4:** a, b, d, d0, dfh, d181, which are not resolved from one another except dfh vs d0/d0f (d181 was paired with d, d0f and b only);
3. **different fine-tune:** e.

Per-arm medians are about 140–900× below the means (INFERRED, ratio of MEASURED values). A small share of positions carries the mean, as in every published KLD table (CLAIMED, [§7.8](#78-external-context-claimed-tagged-as-in-the-methodology-research)).

**About "Q4_K_XL should beat IQ4 in theory".** This is not what was measured, and the premise is weaker than it looks (INFERRED):

**Findings.**
- **vLLM and radiance are (near-)deterministic across re-boots for this request shape** (one sequential client, `max_tokens 1`) (MEASURED). Any difference between two of their arms is therefore a config effect, not boot noise.
- **The llama.cpp floor is the conservative one.** The reference rows come from greedy *decode* steps, while q8f, like every arm, is scored from a *prefill* of the same context. So q8f carries the decode-vs-prefill numerics difference that every arm carries too (INFERRED). The engine audit checked that this path difference adds no bias: KL(q8‖dfh) = 0.0562 vs KL(q8f‖dfh) = 0.0563 (MEASURED, engine audit Q6).
- **The floor is a heavy tail.** Its median is 2e-6 against a mean of 1.8e-3, and the top 1% of positions carries 83% of it (MEASURED, engine audit Q7). The floor lives at near-tie positions, not as a uniform shift (INFERRED).
- **Plausibility.** The 0.0018 floor sits at the published "same weights, different implementation" level of ~1e-3 (CLAIMED, thinkingmachines.ai, T2). It is also about 3× llama.cpp's own f16-storage floor of 5.5e-4 (CLAIMED, llama.cpp `tools/perplexity/README.md`, T1).
- **Superseded audit statement.** The engine audit estimated a "vLLM floor" of 0.0097. That figure is the d-vs-d0 pairwise KL, and d and d0 are **different configs** (DFlash loaded, MAXLEN 260000 vs 110000). The d0f re-boot shows the vLLM boot floor is about 0. The 0.0097 is a config effect, discussed in §5.
- **Not measured:** a *cross-engine* floor (the same weights on two engines). No such pair exists in this batch.

## 5. DFlash and MTP (speculative decoding)

**Forced mode cannot see drafting.**
A forced request ends after one prefill-sampled token (`max_tokens 1`), so no draft is verified (INFERRED, engine audit Q3). The forced d − d0 difference, +0.0031 [−0.0002, +0.0065], is therefore **not resolved**: d is lower in 39% of prompts (MEASURED, `diff2-d-d0`; `diff2-d-d0f` is identical).

d and d0 still do not produce identical rows: pairwise KL(d0‖d) is 0.0097, 0/4096 rows are bit-identical, and top-1 agreement is 97.9% (MEASURED, engine audit Q3). The d0f re-boot is bit-identical to d0 in 4083 of 4096 rows (MEASURED, raw local file, not committed, [§12.6](#126-numbers-derived-from-raw-local-files-not-committed)), so that gap is a **config effect**. The candidates are DFlash loaded, MAXLEN 260000 vs 110000, and possibly a different runner path; the cause is UNKNOWN. That config effect is not resolved against Q8_0.

## 7. Methodology and audit

### 7.1 What is considered, and why

We measure how far each quant or engine moves the model's next-token probabilities away from a Q8_0 reference, using KL divergence. This is the "information lost per token" metric that llama.cpp's own `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence` reports and that most published quant comparisons lead with (CLAIMED, llama.cpp `tools/perplexity/README.md`, T1).

Several engines return only their top 20 candidates. So the divergence is computed on that top-20 slice plus one lumped remainder. This approximates the full-vocabulary value, slightly on the low side ([§7.3](#73-the-top-20-lower-bound-caveat)). Published tests of the same idea preserve rankings (CLAIMED, smcleod.net 2026-04, T3).

To separate real differences from noise, the reference model is run a second time on the same engine (q8f). Each other engine is also re-booted once (d0f, bf). Arms are compared position by position on identical contexts, with a bootstrap that resamples whole prompts. Neighbouring positions in one prompt are correlated, and a handful of hard positions dominate the average.

The mean is reported together with the median, top-1 agreement and dNLL. Near-lossless quants sit in a "silent zone" where KLD no longer tracks task accuracy (CLAIMED, arXiv 2606.19558, T2 preprint). So KLD is used to rule out lossy configurations and to price engine and config drift. Close calls go to the task benchmarks. This paragraph adapts the methodology research's paste-ready text, updated with the per-engine floors.

### 7.2 How it is measured

- **Reference.**
  - Model and engine: unsloth Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 on llama.cpp b11448-ab09ea4 (Vulkan, f16 KV, ctx 32768, 1 slot, `-fa on -ub 2048 -b 8192`).
  - Fetch: a temperature-0 greedy continuation of up to 512 tokens, with top-20 logprobs, for the 128 calibration-v2 prompts. The prompts are raw text with no template applied by the server.
  - Shape (MEASURED, raw local file, not committed: `tmp/runs/forced-20261112-093746/results/kld-q8.jsonl`, fields `prompt_tokens`, `completion_tokens`, `finish_reason`): prompts are 246–4681 tokens (median 982), and continuations are 55–512 tokens (median 512), 55,068 tokens in total. 91 continuations hit the 512 cap (`length`) and 37 ended on their own (`stop`); 0 errors.
  - Key: `kld-q8.jsonl.key.json` (n 128, max_tokens 512, top_k 20).
- **Arm requests (teacher-forced).**
  - Each arm gets the prompt ids plus the reference's first *i* generated ids as an int-list `/v1/completions` prompt.
  - This is done at **32 evenly spaced *i* per prompt**, with `max_tokens 1, temperature 0, logprobs 20`.
  - The forced path checks `usage.prompt_tokens == len(sent ids)` on every request, which would catch BOS insertion or re-tokenisation (CLAIMED, `kld_probe.py`, per audit Q6). The result was 0 errors in all arms.
- **Scoring** (`kld_probe.py score_forced`).
  - At each position, KL(ref‖arm) is computed over the reference top-20 as support. A reference token missing from the arm's top-20 is scored at the arm's lowest top-20 logprob (the floor). One lumped tail bucket takes 1 − Σtop-20 on each side. The result is clamped ≥ 0.
  - Before scoring, both rows pass `canon_top` ([§7.5](#75-scorer-fix-2026-11-12)).
  - Per arm: mean over all 4096 positions, with a cluster-bootstrap 95% CI (block = prompt, B = 2000, percentile).
  - Also: top-1 agreement after canonicalisation; dNLL of the reference token; median, p90 and max per position; `ref_missing_frac`, the share of reference top-20 entries absent from the arm's top-20.
- **Paired differences** (`score_forced_diff`). KL(ref‖a) − KL(ref‖b) position by position on identical contexts, with a cluster bootstrap of the per-position difference.
  - The verdict is "resolved" only when the CI excludes 0.
  - Both arms must have been forced at the same positions (asserted).
- **Errors.** An errored prompt in either arm is excluded and counted (rule 18). There were 0 in every final score.

### 7.3 The top-20 lower-bound caveat

The estimator is a **lower bound in intent** (the reference-top-20 support and the floor substitution both under-count). Strictly, it is only a lower bound when both sides use the same partition. Here the arm's tail bucket is built from the arm's own top-20 set. When the two sets differ, that term can over-count, so the estimator is "approximately a lower bound" (INFERRED, coarse-graining argument, methodology research Q2.1).

- **Size of the bias.** A K sweep from another tool shows the lumped-tail bias at −6.6% for K=64, shrinking with K. Extrapolated to K=20, the bias is roughly −5 to −15% on the mean, with the same sign for every arm (CLAIMED data, smcleod.net T3; extrapolation INFERRED). This is not a correction factor.
- **The clamp at 0.** It makes noise-only positions one-sided positive, so the floor rows are slightly inflated (INFERRED).
- **Unequal row completeness across engines.** Arm top-20 rows can come back short:

  | Engine | Rows short of 20 | Cause |
  |---|---|---|
  | Radiance | 9.2–9.8% | legacy-dict collisions on the server side |
  | vLLM | 3.1–3.2% | dict keyed by string |
  | llama.cpp | 0% | — |

  MEASURED, `arm_topk_short` in `kldf2-*`. These rows get a slightly different floor and tail term. The effect is small, because the missing entries are low-mass (INFERRED, engine audit Q7).
- **Absolute values are not portable.** Our corpus is a greedy continuation of code, prose and chat cut mid-text. Absolute KLD depends on corpus, model and tool. Published Q8_0-vs-BF16 values range from 1.4e-3 to 7.5e-2 across setups (CLAIMED, methodology research Q1.2). Compare these numbers only with each other, never with `llama-perplexity` or Unsloth tables (INFERRED).

### 7.4 Positions rule

`POSITION_RULE = skip-partial-utf8-v1`. It drops a reference position when the chosen token or its top-1 alternative is a partial UTF-8 byte (`'�'`). The remaining 32 positions are evenly spaced over what is left.

Two reasons:
- llama.cpp answers `max_tokens 1` at such a position with **no logprobs row**, because it holds the incomplete character back.
- Every partial byte prints as the same `'�'` string, so it cannot be matched across engines.

Both reasons are CLAIMED by the `partial_utf8_positions` docstring and MEASURED by the stale first q8f fetch: 4 of 128 prompts errored "no logprobs row at position N" (MEASURED, raw local file, not committed: `tmp/runs/forced-20261112-093746/results/kld-q8f.jsonl.stale-20261112-121530`). The rule excludes 161 of 54,852 reference positions (0.29%), the same set for every arm (MEASURED, engine audit Q7). Every forced key file records `"position_rule": "skip-partial-utf8-v1"` ([§12.3](#123-calibration-and-cache-pins)).

### 7.5 Scorer fix (2026-11-12)

The first scoring of this batch gave a **q8f floor of 0.1106**: a Q8_0 re-run looked 60× noisier than it is (MEASURED, engine audit Q5). Two string-keying artifacts caused it:
1. **Duplicate strings within one top-20.** Several entries rendered as `''` collided as dict keys.
2. **Control specials render differently by engine.** llama.cpp renders them as `''`, while vLLM and radiance render them as text, e.g. `'<|im_end|>'`. Every end-of-turn position was charged about 20 nats (CLAIMED, `canon_tok` docstring). The orchestrator's session analysis found 98% of the pre-fix total in 1% of positions; that analysis is not committed, so this figure is not re-verified here.

**Fix.** `canon_tok` maps `''` and every `<|…|>` / `<tts_…>` special to one `<special>` bucket. `canon_top` merges entries that share a canonical string by logsumexp, on both sides. This is a coarser event space applied identically to both sides. Non-special added tokens (`<think>`, `<tool_call>`) keep their text.

**Effect** (MEASURED, engine audit Q5):

| Arm | Before | After |
|---|--:|--:|
| q8f | 0.1106 | 0.00176 |
| d | 0.167 | 0.0638 |

The bucket term itself contributes only +0.0014 to d.

The fix cannot undo collisions the server already made (vLLM keeps the *less* likely of two equal strings; radiance's legacy dict keeps the likelier). That is why token-ID keying is the next step ([§10](#10-limitations-and-next-steps)). The batch was scored with the fixed `kld_probe.py` while it was still uncommitted on top of `2ef0016`; the fix (with `test_kld_probe.py`) was committed in `79d43f0`. The pre-fix values in the table above come from the engine audit (committed research file), not from a committed score file.

## 8. What KLD means, in plain words

A longer version for non-technical readers, with an analogy and a reading guide, is the first part of the campaign README's [KLD section](../../../campaigns/perf/2026-11-09-1825-27b-arms-kld/README.md#kld-ws3). This is the short version.

**The idea.**
Before a model writes each token, it scores every token it could write next, for example "`return` 70%, `if` 20%, `for` 5%, …". A quantized model is a compressed copy, and it should give almost the same scores as the original. KLD (Kullback–Leibler divergence) is one number that says how far apart two of those score lists are.

**How to read the number.**
0 means both models think exactly alike at that step, and bigger means they disagree more. The unit is "nats per token", averaged over many steps. To give a sense of scale in *this* test (MEASURED, §2):

| What | KL nats/token |
|---|--:|
| The same Q8_0 model run twice | about 0.002 (0.0018). This is pure measurement noise. |
| The best 4-bit copy (IQ4R-L-V4, arm c) | about 0.008 |
| A good llama.cpp 4-bit copy (UD-Q4_K_XL) | about 0.016 |
| The MXFP4 4-bit copies, on any engine | 0.056–0.066 |
| A different fine-tune (heretic) | about 0.089 |

The average is pulled up by a few hard steps. The median step is over 100× smaller than the average.

![Median, mean and 90th percentile KL per arm, log scale](../../../campaigns/perf/2026-11-09-1825-27b-arms-kld/results/kld-final/charts/kld-typical-vs-mean.svg)

*Figure 3. For every arm the median position (open circle) sits far to the left of the mean (filled dot), so most positions agree almost exactly and a few large misses carry the score. The mean is still the right score because KL is defined as an average over positions, and the rare large misses are the cases where an arm really diverges from Q8_0, which a median would hide (INFERRED).*

**Top-1 agreement, the easier number.**
Top-1 agreement is how often both models put the *same* token first. 98.7% (IQ4R) means they would pick a different next token about once every 80 steps. 95.5% (MXFP4) means about once every 22 steps. Both figures are INFERRED as 1 / (1 − agreement).

**How we measure it here.**
1. We take 128 fixed text snippets, cut mid-way: 90 code, 14 prose and 24 chat turns. The set is hash-pinned in `inputs/`.
2. The reference, Q8_0 (a near-original copy), continues each snippet with its most likely token every time, for up to 512 tokens. At each step it records its top 20 candidates with their scores.
3. Every other model is then shown the same snippet plus the reference's first *i* tokens, and asked only for its scores for the next token. This happens at 32 evenly spaced points per snippet ("teacher-forced"). Every model is judged on exactly the same contexts, so the numbers line up across models.
4. At each point we compare the two top-20 lists and compute the KL. Tokens outside the top 20 are counted with a floor value. Control tokens such as end-of-turn are compared as one shared "special" token, because engines spell them differently. The result is roughly a lower bound: the full gap can only be a bit larger.
5. We average over all 4096 points. We get a 95% range by resampling whole snippets (a cluster bootstrap). To compare two models, we take the difference at each point first, then resample. That cancels the "this snippet is hard for everyone" part, so small real differences show up.

**What we compare against.**
- Each engine's own noise: the same model re-run on llama.cpp, and re-booted on vLLM and radiance.
- A difference whose 95% range includes zero is "not resolved". The data cannot tell the two apart. Here, engine-version changes of +0.002 were not resolved, while the int4 lm head's 0.0045 was; what decides is the paired range, not the size alone.

**What speed tricks do.**
Speed tricks such as DFlash or MTP guess several tokens ahead and keep only the guesses the model agrees with. They should not change the scores. A teacher-forced request asks for one token only, so these tricks never run in it. Their effect was therefore checked separately, by letting the model write freely with the trick on and off (§5).

**What KLD does not tell you.**
It measures closeness to the reference, not whether the code is correct. A small KLD means "behaves like Q8_0", and Q8_0 is itself not the original full-precision model. Task scores (tests passing) are a separate measurement, and for close calls they are the one that decides.

## 10. Limitations and next steps

1. **Token-ID keying in `kld_probe.py`.** Today entries are joined by string, and `canon_top` is a sound fallback for data already fetched. Server-side collisions are unrecoverable, though: radiance and vLLM return 3–10% short rows. Each engine can return ids:
   - llama.cpp: keep `id` in `_topk_entries`;
   - radiance: prefer the `content[]` array, which has `id` and `bytes`;
   - vLLM: `return_tokens_as_token_ids`.

   Also send the reference's own prompt ids to every arm and assert id equality, not just length (engine audit, fixes 2–3). Existing fetches do not need a re-run for this alone (MEASURED adequate, audit Q5).
2. **Speed moves to its own campaign.** The plan is [`notes/plans/2026-11-12-1200-27b-arms-speed-campaign-plan.md`](../../../notes/plans/2026-11-12-1200-27b-arms-speed-campaign-plan.md) (approved): the same arms, ≥4 depths with log-log exponents (rule 16), and a growing multi-turn conversation (rule 17). The speed rows from this batch and the compare batch are pilot data only.
3. **The d vs d0 config effect is unexplained.** The pairwise KL of 0.0097 has 0 bit-identical rows (§5). Recording the runner (V1/V2) per boot and booting d0 at MAXLEN 260000 would attribute it. It is not resolved against Q8_0, so it changes no ranking.
4. **There is no greedy self-run control for DFlash.** A d0-vs-d0f free-running pair would show whether 0.0059 is DFlash or greedy nondeterminism.
5. **The KLD is short-context only.** The prompts are ≤4681 tokens (MEASURED, raw local file, not committed, §12.6). Long-context KLD, where quant and KV-type effects may grow, is not measured, and nothing here should be extrapolated to depth.
6. **There is no cross-engine floor.** Running the same weights on two engines (e.g. one GGUF on llama.cpp Vulkan and ROCm) would price pure engine drift.
7. **Q8_0 is the reference, not BF16.** These numbers are distances from Q8_0. On other corpora Q8_0 itself sits 1e-3 to 7.5e-2 from BF16 (CLAIMED, methodology research Q1.2).
8. **The quality decision needs task scores.** c and q4kxl are both near Q8_0 and may sit in the silent zone. The TS quality harness should decide between them (INFERRED from CLAIMED arXiv 2606.19558).
9. **Dropped by the user:** q4mtp (not measurable, §5), c0, and all depth cells. (q6k was dropped from the first batch and measured later in round 3, item 11.)
10. **Heretic: fine-tune vs quant recipe.** Round 2 (done, §1) shows e's extra distance is not the engine. It is still the fine-tune and its MXFP4-GPTQ quantization together; a non-heretic GPTQ build or a higher-precision heretic build would split them (INFERRED).
