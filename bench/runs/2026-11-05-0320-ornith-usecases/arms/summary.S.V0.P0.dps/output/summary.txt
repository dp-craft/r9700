GOAL: Benchmark the deep-context 35B-A3B model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) on an AMD R9700 over Vulkan out to its native 262144-token ceiling, sweeping depth, MTP, KV type, and parallelism.
RESULT: On this box VRAM is not the limit — the RoPE cap is, since f16 KV runs cleanly to 200K with no OOM — so keep f16 (q8_0 KV is a catastrophic, depth-amplified −40% prefill / +66% TTFT). MTP's decode gain grows with depth (+35% @200K) but is a net wall-time LOSS for deep-prefill/short-decode requests and only wins when generation dominates prefill.
KEY: 1254 tok/s | prefill throughput at 200K tokens (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Vulkan R9700, f16 KV, MTP off), down from 3077 at 8K
KEY: 61 tok/s | decode throughput at 200K tokens (same config), down from 104 at 8K
KEY: 285.5 s | TTFT for q8_0 KV cache at 200K vs 171.7 s for f16 (+66%, −40% prefill) — rejected
KEY: -17 s | net wall-time loss of MTP on @ 200K deep-prefill/short-decode (+18 s prefill cost vs +1 s decode saved)
KEY: 49.4 s | TTFT p95 with np=4 parallel 32K prompts vs 24.3 s at np=2, for ~0 aggregate gain (5.5 -> 6.0 tok/s)
CAVEAT: The parallel-throughput conclusion rests on early-EOS prompts that under-measure real aggregate decode scaling at depth (flagged as a follow-up), and the q8_0 result is a single confirmatory point, plus VRAM was not re-sampled this run.
OPEN: Whether a future Mesa/llama.cpp build narrows the Vulkan q8_0 KV kernel penalty that drives the -40% prefill regression at depth.
